Install it (about a minute, only once)

  1. Unzip the file. Double-click utm-now-extension.zip in your Downloads folder. You'll get a folder with the same name. Move it somewhere it can stay, like Documents, and don't delete it afterwards.
  2. Open Chrome's extensions page. Paste chrome://extensions into Chrome's address bar and press Enter.
  3. Turn on "Developer mode". It's the switch at the top right of that page.
  4. Click "Load unpacked". Pick the unzipped folder, then press Select.
  5. Pin it. Click the puzzle-piece icon at the top right of Chrome, then the pin next to UTM-NOW, so it's always one click away.

If Chrome ever shows a warning about developer mode extensions, that's normal for extensions installed this way. Just keep UTM-NOW enabled.

Use it

  1. Open the page you want to share. This can be your website, a blog post, a product page or anything else.
  2. Click the UTM-NOW icon. The page's link is filled in for you.
  3. Fill in Campaign, Medium and Source. The grey examples under each box show what to type.
  4. Click "Copy tagged URL". The link is now on your clipboard. Paste it into your email, post or ad.

About UTM parameters

UTM parameters are small tags added to the end of a link, like ?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=spring_sale. They don't change where the link goes. When someone clicks, your analytics tool (Google Analytics, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo and most others) reads the tags and files that visit under the right campaign.

Without them, a click from your newsletter, a LinkedIn post and a paid ad can all land in the same "direct" or "referral" bucket, and you can't tell which one worked.

The three required parameters

  • utm_source: who or what sent the visitor. A site, platform or list, such as newsletter, linkedin or google.
  • utm_medium: the kind of channel it travelled through, such as email, social, cpc or print.
  • utm_campaign: the initiative the link belongs to, such as spring_sale or product_launch.

The optional ones

  • utm_id: an ID you can match against your own records or ad platform.
  • utm_term: the keyword you bid on in paid search.
  • utm_content: a label for telling apart links in the same campaign, such as header_button vs footer_link, or ad A vs ad B.

This builder works for any analytics tool that reads UTM parameters. Everything is built in your browser, so your links are never sent anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Are the optional parameters worth filling in?

Sometimes. utm_content is the most useful: it lets you A/B test two buttons or two ad versions inside one campaign. utm_term only matters for paid search, and utm_id only if you need to match clicks to something in another system. If in doubt, skip them.

What's the difference between source and medium?

Source is the specific place, medium is the type of place. linkedin and instagram are different sources but share the medium social. That way you can look at all social together, or drill down to one platform.

Should I use lowercase or uppercase?

Lowercase, always. Analytics tools treat LinkedIn and linkedin as two different sources, so your reports split in two. Pick one style (for example lowercase with underscores, no spaces) and have your whole team use it.

Can I put UTM links on links inside my own website?

Avoid it. A UTM tag on an internal link can start a new session and overwrite the original source of the visit. Use UTMs on links that bring people to your site: emails, social posts, ads, partner pages and printed material.

Does it matter if the link already has a ? in it?

No. Paste the link as it is. Existing parameters and any #section at the end are kept, and the UTM tags are added in the right place.
